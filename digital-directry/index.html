One Platform.
Four Connected Portals.

A connected local discovery and collaboration ecosystem that brings users, local businesses, influencers and platform administrators together through AI-powered discovery, business visibility, promotional campaigns and measurable engagement.

01 / Project overview

What are we building?

The platform connects people searching for local services with businesses that want visibility, while giving influencers a structured way to collaborate with businesses and other creators.

01

Discover

Users ask questions through an AI-style chat interface and discover relevant local businesses, services and offers.

Example: A user asks, “Find me a barber near me.” The platform returns relevant local barber shops.
02

Promote

Vendors manage their business profiles, publish content, receive visibility and create promotional offers for influencers.

Example: A restaurant offers a creator 15% commission or a free meal in exchange for agreed promotional content.
03

Collaborate

Influencers discover vendor campaigns, connect with other creators and publish their own collaboration opportunities.

Example: Two food creators connect to produce a joint restaurant review video.
Core concept: The User App generates discovery and engagement. The Vendor App monetizes business visibility and campaigns. The Influencer App enables promotional collaborations. The Admin Portal controls the complete ecosystem.

03 / Admin web portal

The central control room

Admin has visibility and management authority over all activities across the Vendor, Influencer and User Apps. It is the operational backbone of the platform.

1. Dashboard & platform overview

Shows total registered users, active vendors, influencers, subscriptions, revenue, credits consumed, searches and campaign activity.

Example: Admin sees 1,200 users, 180 vendors, 75 influencers and 24 active campaigns. These are illustrative dashboard figures.

2. User management

View user profiles, registration details, activity, saved businesses, chat history metadata and account status. Admin can suspend or restore accounts.

Example: Support receives a complaint and checks the user's account status and relevant activity.

3. Vendor management & approvals

Review vendor registrations, verify business details, approve or reject applications, edit categories and manage business status.

Example: A new salon submits its business registration. Admin verifies its details before publishing its profile.

4. Existing business claim requests

Manage requests from businesses that already exist on Google Business Profile or another supported business directory.

Example: An existing restaurant requests ownership of its listing. Admin checks business ownership before linking it to the vendor account.

5. External business lead management

Review businesses discovered by the User App that are not yet registered on the platform. Admin can assign leads to the onboarding team and track follow-ups.

Example: A user finds a barber that is not a platform vendor. The business is saved as an external lead for the sales team.

6. Categories and locations

Create and manage business categories, subcategories, service types, locations and discovery filters.

Example: Admin creates Food & Dining, then subcategories such as Restaurants, Cafes and Bakeries.

7. Credit management and ledger

Configure free credits, paid credit packages, deduction rules, expiry, adjustments, refunds and vendor transaction history.

Example: A vendor starts with 500 free credits. Each eligible engagement deducts credits according to the configured rate.

8. Subscription and plan management

Create vendor and influencer plans, trial periods, prices, renewal rules, feature limits and subscription statuses.

Example: Admin creates a 14-day vendor trial followed by a monthly paid plan.

9. Payments and financial management

Monitor invoices, payment gateway references, subscription payments, credit purchases, refunds, fees, taxes and transaction statuses.

Example: Finance checks whether a vendor's monthly subscription payment was successful or failed.

10. Influencer campaign management

Monitor vendor offers, creator applications, approvals, deliverables, collaboration statuses and disputes.

Example: Admin checks a campaign where a creator accepted an offer but has not submitted the agreed video.

11. Influencer payments and settlements

If payments are processed through the platform, manage payout requests, balances, payment references, fees and settlement statuses. If vendors pay creators directly, record the agreed amount and confirmation without treating it as a platform payout.

Example: A vendor agrees to pay a creator $100. Admin can track whether the agreement was completed.

12. Advertisement management

Create sponsored banners and popups, choose target audiences, schedule campaigns and set frequency limits.

Example: Admin runs a sponsored restaurant promotion for users searching in a specific location.

13. Social media integrations

Monitor connected social accounts, publishing permissions, expired tokens, failed uploads and scheduled content.

Example: Admin can see that a vendor's Instagram connection needs to be re-authorized.

14. AI and search configuration

Configure AI provider settings, prompts, search sources, result limits, location logic and business matching rules.

Example: Admin changes the default number of business results returned from 10 to 15.

15. Booking and inquiry management

View booking requests, contact inquiries, appointment statuses and integration errors.

Example: Admin checks a booking request that was sent to a vendor but has not yet been confirmed.

16. Reviews and content moderation

Moderate platform-generated content, reported profiles, in-app reviews and inappropriate images or promotional material.

Example: Admin reviews a reported vendor image before deciding whether to remove it.

17. Notifications and announcements

Send system notifications, email alerts, push notifications and announcements to selected user groups.

Example: Admin announces a new campaign feature to all registered influencers.

18. Roles, permissions and audit logs

Give different staff members controlled access and maintain a record of important administrative actions.

Example: Finance can access payment records, while Vendor Onboarding can approve business profiles.

19. Reports and analytics

Generate daily, weekly and monthly reports for platform activity, vendors, influencers, users, revenue and campaigns.

Example: Admin compares vendor profile opens this month with the previous month.

20. Customer support and disputes

Track user complaints, vendor support requests, influencer disputes and account-related issues.

Example: An influencer reports that a vendor did not honor an accepted campaign agreement.

21. Data exports

Export permitted records and reports to CSV or Excel for accounting, analysis and operations.

Example: Finance exports monthly subscription transactions.

22. Platform settings

Manage global configuration, currencies, time zones, trial rules, terms, privacy settings and feature switches.

Example: Admin changes the default trial duration for newly registered vendors.

23. System health and integrations

Monitor API failures, search provider availability, payment gateway errors, notification delivery and background jobs.

Example: Admin sees that a social publishing job failed and can review the error.

24. Data privacy and user requests

Manage consent records and approved requests for data export, correction or deletion according to platform policy.

Example: A user requests account deletion. The request is recorded and processed under the privacy policy.
04 / Vendor application

Vendor App — Business growth dashboard

The Vendor App allows business owners to register, manage their listing, track visibility, spend credits, publish content and collaborate with influencers.

1. Vendor registration and onboarding

New business owners complete a registration form with business name, owner details, contact information, category, location, address, services, opening hours, images and supporting information.

Example: A new barber shop owner creates an account and submits the business profile for approval.

2. Existing business claim

Businesses already listed on Google Business Profile can request to claim or connect their existing listing. The system should verify ownership before granting management access.

Example: Two existing vendors are already listed on Google. They can search for their business and submit a claim request instead of creating duplicate listings.

3. Vendor profile management

Manage business name, description, logo, cover image, contact details, address, map location, categories, services, products, offers and business hours.

Example: A restaurant updates its menu, contact number and weekend opening hours.

4. Business verification and approval status

Vendors can see whether their profile is pending, approved, rejected, suspended or requires additional information.

Example: A vendor sees “Pending Verification” and uploads the missing business document.

5. Free credits and trial balance

Every eligible new vendor receives a configured number of free credits. The balance decreases as users interact with the vendor's listing.

Example: A vendor receives 500 free credits and can track the remaining balance from the dashboard.

6. Credit deduction rules

Different actions can have different credit rates. A listing appearing in a user's chat can have one rate, while opening the full vendor profile costs twice that rate.

Example: If a chat appearance costs 2 credits, a full profile open costs 4 credits. These rates are configurable by Admin.

7. Credit purchase and subscription

After free credits or trial limits are reached, vendors can select a paid plan or purchase additional credits, depending on the platform's commercial model.

Example: A vendor receives a low-balance notification and purchases a credit package to continue receiving paid visibility.

8. Visibility and engagement analytics

Show total chat appearances, unique appearances, profile views, unique profile visitors, clicks, calls, website visits and other CTA actions.

Example: A vendor can see that its listing appeared 860 times, received 210 profile opens and generated 94 contact clicks.

9. Daily, weekly and monthly reports

Vendors can select a date range and view engagement and credit consumption for that period.

Example: A restaurant compares this week's profile views with last week's results.

10. Social media account connections

Connect supported business social accounts through official authorization flows. The app should not ask users to share their social passwords.

Example: A vendor connects an eligible Instagram or Facebook account through the official connection process.

11. Social media content publishing

Upload images and videos, select channels, prepare captions and schedule or publish content according to each channel's supported features and limits.

Example: A vendor uploads a promotional image and selects the connected channels where it can be published.

12. Publishing queue and status

Show scheduled, published, failed and pending posts. Provide retry options and alerts when an account connection expires.

Example: A failed post remains in the queue with its error reason for the vendor to review.

13. Influencer offer creation

Vendors can create promotional campaigns with a title, description, category, requirements, deliverables and application deadline.

Example: A cafe creates an offer for local food influencers to review its new menu.

14. Offer compensation options

Choose percentage commission, fixed payment, free products or services, barter, or a combination of compensation methods.

Example: A restaurant offers a free meal plus 10% commission on tracked referrals.

15. Influencer category targeting

Choose the categories of influencers eligible to see a campaign. The maximum number of selected categories should be configurable, for example 3 or 5.

Example: A restaurant selects Food, Lifestyle and Local Travel. Only creators matching eligible categories see the offer.

16. Applicant management

View interested creators, check their profiles and social links, compare applications and approve or reject applicants.

Example: Five creators apply for a campaign. The vendor selects two and rejects the others.

17. Campaign tracking

Track accepted creators, agreed deliverables, deadlines, submitted content and completion status.

Example: A vendor checks whether the accepted creator has published the agreed promotional video.
05 / Influencer application

Influencer App — Creator collaboration network

The Influencer App gives creators a dedicated place to build their profiles, discover business opportunities, connect with other influencers and manage collaboration requests.

1. Influencer registration

Creators register with their name, email, contact details, location, profile image and creator information.

Example: A Karachi-based food creator creates an account and selects Food & Dining as a primary category.

2. Social media profiles

Add public social profile URLs and, where supported, connect social accounts to retrieve authorized account information.

Example: A creator adds Instagram, TikTok and YouTube profile links to their creator profile.

3. Influencer profile

Display creator biography, niche, location, audience information, portfolio, public links and collaboration history.

Example: A food influencer shows their niche, previous restaurant collaborations and portfolio links.

4. Vendor collaboration feed

Creators discover campaigns created by vendors according to their selected categories and eligibility.

Example: A food creator sees restaurant campaigns, while a beauty creator sees salon and skincare offers.

5. Swipe-based discovery

Provide a swipe-style interface for browsing vendor campaign cards. Creators can express interest, skip or open the complete offer details.

Example: A creator swipes right on a restaurant offer to express interest and left to skip it.

6. Apply for vendor offers

Creators submit an application or express interest. Vendors then review and decide whether to accept or reject the application.

Example: A creator applies for a paid cafe promotion. The vendor receives the application in the Vendor App.

7. Application and acceptance counts

Show interested or applied creators separately from creators actually accepted by the vendor.

Example: A campaign displays “18 interested” and “3 accepted.” These numbers represent different stages.

8. Influencer-to-influencer collaboration

Creators can discover other creators who want to collaborate based on niche, location, interests and collaboration preferences.

Example: Two travel creators connect to plan a joint city exploration video.

9. Creator collaboration posts

Allow an influencer to publish their own collaboration request with a title, category, location, requirements and preferred creator type.

Example: A creator publishes “Looking for a food photographer in Karachi for a joint project.”

10. Swipe-based creator discovery

Use creator profile cards to help influencers discover and express interest in other creators.

Example: A travel creator sees a compatible local photographer and sends a collaboration request.

11. Collaboration request management

Creators can accept, reject or manage incoming requests and see the status of their outgoing requests.

Example: A creator receives a collaboration request and accepts it from the notification screen.

12. Notifications

Notify creators when a vendor offer matches them, a vendor accepts their application, another creator contacts them or a collaboration status changes.

Example: “Your application for the Cafe Promotion campaign has been accepted.”

13. Collaboration dashboard

Show total applications, interested offers, accepted collaborations, pending requests, completed projects and rejected applications.

Example: A creator sees 12 applications, 4 accepted collaborations and 2 completed projects.

14. Trial and paid subscription

Influencers receive a configured trial and can upgrade to a paid plan for additional discovery, applications or platform features.

Example: A creator uses the trial plan and upgrades after reaching the plan's application limit.

06 / User application

User App — AI-powered local discovery

The User App provides a conversational search experience. Users can ask questions naturally and discover relevant local businesses, services and promotional offers.

1. ChatGPT-style interface

A simple chat interface where users type natural-language questions instead of filling traditional search forms.

Example: “Find me a good barber near me that is open today.”

2. AI-powered query understanding

The backend AI understands the user's intent, category, location, preferences and relevant filters.

Example: The system understands that “good barber near me” means a nearby barber business search.

3. External business data discovery

Search connected data providers, directories or permitted data sources such as Apify-based workflows to discover relevant businesses not already stored in the platform database.

Example: The search process discovers 10 barber shops in the user's selected area.

4. Database matching

Match discovered businesses with existing vendor records using identifiers and business details to avoid duplicate records.

Example: If a discovered barber shop is already an approved platform vendor, display its verified platform profile.

5. External business lead creation

If a business is not a registered vendor, save it as an external listing or onboarding lead for Admin review. Do not present an unclaimed external listing as a verified platform vendor.

Example: A user selects a barber shop found externally. Admin receives the business details as a potential vendor lead.

6. Business results

Display relevant results with business names, categories, locations, available images, source information and supported details.

Example: The user receives 10 relevant barber shop results with available information.

7. Business profile details

Show the vendor's description, services, products, offers, images, contact details, opening hours and available reviews.

Example: A user opens a restaurant profile and checks its menu, images, location and current offer.

07 / Credit and billing logic

How vendor credits work

Credits measure eligible visibility and engagement events. Each billable action should be recorded in a transparent ledger so vendors and Admin can understand every deduction.

01

Chat appearance

When a vendor's listing is displayed in relevant AI search results, the platform records a qualifying appearance.

Illustrative rate: 2 credits per qualifying appearance.
02

Full profile open

When a user opens the full business profile, a higher engagement charge is applied.

Illustrative rate: 4 credits, which is 2x the 2-credit appearance rate.
03

CTA and contact actions

Call, website, booking and message actions are tracked separately. Whether these actions consume credits is a configurable commercial decision.

Example: Admin can choose to charge for a qualified call click or simply include it in analytics.
Important billing protection: Refreshes, repeated requests, automated bots and duplicate events must not create accidental deductions. Each billable event needs a unique event ID, clear eligibility rules and a visible vendor ledger. Admin should be able to investigate and reverse incorrect deductions.

10 / Shared platform foundation

Core systems behind all portals

All four portals should use a shared backend rather than storing disconnected copies of the same business and user information.

Central database

Stores users, vendors, influencers, business listings, categories, campaigns, applications, credits, subscriptions, payments, bookings and activity records.

Authentication and permissions

Each person logs in with the correct role and can access only the functions and data permitted for that role.

AI and search engine

Processes natural-language questions, finds relevant businesses, ranks results and uses verified data wherever possible.

Credit and billing engine

Calculates eligible usage, records deductions and purchases, and maintains an auditable transaction ledger.

Notification engine

Delivers push, email and in-app notifications for account, campaign, payment and booking events.

Integration layer

Connects permitted external search providers, social networks, maps, payment gateways and booking services.

11 / Important product rules

Decisions and safeguards

These rules protect platform accuracy, billing transparency, user privacy and operational reliability.

Business data accuracy

External listings and verified platform vendors must be clearly distinguished. Business hours, prices, reviews and availability should not be invented by AI.

External data compliance

Search providers and data extraction services must be used according to their terms, permitted access, attribution and privacy requirements.

Credit billing transparency

Define billable events, unique event handling, bot filtering, duplicate protection, credit expiry and refund or adjustment procedures.

Social media limitations

Publishing depends on official API permissions, supported media formats, account eligibility, rate limits and channel-specific rules.

Influencer agreements

Record compensation, content requirements, deadlines, cancellation, proof of delivery and dispute status for every accepted collaboration.

Reviews and ratings

Clearly identify external reviews and platform-native reviews. Preserve their original source and avoid mixing them as if they were collected in the same way.

Bookings and appointments

Clearly distinguish a booking request from a confirmed booking. Confirmation requires a vendor response or a successful booking-system integration.

Privacy and location

Request location permission only when needed. Provide manual location selection and clear controls for history, consent and account data requests.

Sponsored advertising

Clearly label paid placements and use frequency caps so users are not overwhelmed by repeated banners or popups.

Staff access and audit

Use role-based permissions and log important actions such as approvals, refunds, credit adjustments and account suspensions.
