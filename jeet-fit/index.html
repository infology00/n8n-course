Proposed scope

🧱01. Account & funnel audit

  • Review existing GHL location, domains, forms, calendars, pipelines, workflows and integrations.
  • Map current lead sources and identify duplicate, broken or conflicting automations.
  • Check user roles, ownership, permissions, tracking and data consistency.
  • Document what should be retained, repaired, simplified or newly configured.

🧲02. Lead capture & CRM

  • Configure or refine forms, required fields, source attribution, tags and custom fields.
  • Define duplicate-contact handling and contact update rules.
  • Set lead assignment, notifications, tasks and clear pipeline-stage criteria.
  • Validate form submissions on mobile and desktop, including error states.

⚙️03. Workflow automation

  • New enquiry acknowledgement and internal team notification.
  • Follow-up sequences with wait conditions, response checks and stop rules.
  • Branching for booked, replied, not interested, unreachable and opted-out contacts.
  • Error handling, workflow naming conventions, test contacts and change notes.

💬04. Messaging & integrations

  • Review approved email, SMS and WhatsApp providers and current account status.
  • Map message templates, sender identity, consent, opt-out and delivery failure handling.
  • Connect supported tools via native integration, webhook or API where appropriate.
  • Test authentication, payload mapping, duplicate events and failed requests.

📆05. Appointments & outcomes

  • Calendar settings, booking form fields, confirmation and reminder messages.
  • Reschedule and cancellation pathways, plus no-show and attendance outcomes.
  • Notify the right team member and create follow-up tasks after appointments.
  • Prevent inappropriate reminders after cancellation or completed appointments.

🧠06. AI agent & team handoff

  • Define the assistant’s role, approved knowledge and supported question types.
  • Design qualification questions and a clear path to book or contact staff.
  • Set boundaries for unknown answers, complaints and sensitive enquiries.
  • Test common questions, edge cases, handoff and conversation summaries.

📈07. Dashboards & reporting

  • Agree definitions for new leads, contacted, qualified, booked, attended and converted.
  • Build practical views for sales owners, team leads and management as supported by GHL.
  • Review lead sources, response time, overdue tasks, appointments and pipeline outcomes.
  • Document metric limitations and any external reporting dependency.

🧪08. QA, launch & handover

  • Test the full journey from enquiry to message, booking, outcome and reporting.
  • Test duplicate submissions, invalid data, opt-out, failed integrations and rescheduling.
  • Provide a workflow inventory, configuration notes and maintenance checklist.
  • Walk the team through key processes and define post-launch checks.

JEFT FIT — GoHighLevel, Lead Management & AI Automation

A solution concept for a connected lead capture, follow-up, appointment and sales reporting system.

Prepared for: JEFT FITPrepared by: AWSZDocument type: Concept proposalCommercials: To be discussed in a meeting

1. Executive overview

This proposal outlines a practical GHL-centred system to help JEFT FIT capture enquiries consistently, organize lead ownership, automate appropriate follow-up, reduce missed next steps, support appointment booking, and measure sales activity. The objective is not to add automation for its own sake; it is to create a transparent process that the team can operate and improve.

The first step is an audit of the existing environment. The final configuration should preserve working components wherever practical, avoid unnecessary rebuilds, and only introduce additional integrations when there is a clear operational reason.

2. Objectives & success criteria

  • Reliable capture: a valid enquiry creates or updates the correct contact record and records its source where available.
  • Clear ownership: each actionable lead has a defined owner, pipeline stage and next action.
  • Timely communication: approved acknowledgement and follow-up messages trigger under documented conditions.
  • Controlled automation: a reply, booking, cancellation, opt-out or defined outcome stops or changes the relevant workflow.
  • Appointment visibility: booking status and outcome can be reviewed by the team.
  • Useful reporting: agreed metrics use consistent definitions and can be checked against source records.
  • Maintainability: workflows are named, documented, tested and handed over to the team.

Performance targets should be agreed after the current baseline is reviewed. This concept does not promise a particular conversion lift or business result.

4. Proposed GHL architecture

  • Contact layer: contact fields, source data, communication preferences, goal/intent fields, owner and lifecycle status.
  • Capture layer: GHL forms/funnels or approved existing website forms, with clear thank-you states and tracking where required.
  • Pipeline layer: stages with written entry/exit criteria, such as New Lead → Attempting Contact → Contacted → Qualified → Appointment Booked → Attended → Won / Lost / Nurture.
  • Workflow layer: modular workflows with clear names, entry conditions, wait steps, branch logic, stop conditions and internal alerts.
  • Communication layer: approved email, SMS and WhatsApp connections, using provider-supported functionality and consent rules.
  • Calendar layer: connected calendars, availability, booking confirmations, reminders and appointment outcome handling.
  • AI layer: approved knowledge, narrow task boundaries, qualification logic, booking guidance and human handoff.
  • Reporting layer: dashboards and reports based on available GHL data, with external reporting considered only if a documented gap requires it.

5. Workflow specifications

Workflow A — New lead intake

  1. Trigger on a qualifying form submission or verified inbound lead event.
  2. Validate required fields and capture source, timestamp and consent/preferences where supported.
  3. Create/update contact according to the duplicate-contact rules; avoid creating a second opportunity unnecessarily.
  4. Apply lifecycle/source tags and create or update the opportunity in the agreed pipeline.
  5. Assign the correct owner using the agreed routing rule; set a fallback if no rule matches.
  6. Create a first-response task and send an internal notification with a direct CRM link where supported.
  7. Show a clear confirmation to the prospect and start the approved acknowledgement workflow.

Workflow B — Welcome & follow-up

  1. Confirm the contact is eligible for the selected communication channel.
  2. Send the welcome message once, with accurate personalization and an appropriate booking or reply option.
  3. Wait for a defined event or interval; re-check replies, booking status, contact status and opt-out before each further message.
  4. If the prospect replies, stop automated nudges and create/assign a human response task where required.
  5. If the prospect books, stop the lead-nurture sequence and move into appointment communications.
  6. If the prospect opts out, apply suppression and stop the relevant channel’s marketing communication as required.
  7. If no response is received, continue only the approved cadence and then move to a low-frequency nurture or task review state.

Workflow C — Appointment lifecycle

  1. Trigger on a confirmed booking event from the connected calendar.
  2. Update the opportunity/appointment fields and send confirmation with the correct time zone.
  3. Schedule reminders relative to the appointment time; avoid duplicate reminders.
  4. When rescheduled or cancelled, update the status and cancel or adjust pending reminder steps.
  5. After the appointment, prompt the responsible team member to record the outcome.
  6. Branch to the appropriate next action: follow-up, rebooking, nurture, won or lost.

Workflow D — Sales team accountability

  1. Create tasks for leads that need contact or have a due follow-up.
  2. Track owner, task status, first response timestamp and last contact where available.
  3. Notify a team lead when an agreed task becomes overdue; avoid noisy repeated alerts.
  4. Provide a daily or weekly queue for unassigned leads, overdue tasks and appointments without outcomes.
  5. Review whether pipeline stages and team metrics match the actual sales process.

Workflow E — AI assistant and escalation

  1. Define supported intents: service FAQs, basic qualification, booking guidance and approved policy questions.
  2. Use only JEFT FIT-approved business content; if information is missing, say so and offer staff handoff.
  3. Collect only necessary information and avoid asking for sensitive details that are not needed.
  4. Offer booking when appropriate, but verify availability through the connected calendar rather than guessing.
  5. Escalate complaints, exceptions, uncertain answers or explicit requests for a person.
  6. Log a concise summary and the next action for the team, subject to platform capability and privacy policy.

6. Messaging and integration considerations

The implementation should begin with a capability check of existing accounts and approved providers. WhatsApp may require an eligible provider connection, business verification, templates or opt-in depending on the chosen route. SMS and email delivery depend on valid sender configuration, account status, deliverability and consent. API/webhook integrations should use least-privilege credentials and should never expose secrets in front-end code.

  • Confirm which channel is authoritative for each message and prevent the same notification from being sent twice.
  • Map fields and normalize phone numbers, time zones, source values and status labels.
  • Use authenticated webhooks/API calls, validate payloads and define retry/failure behavior.
  • Keep API keys, tokens and private credentials server-side or in the platform’s secure credential store.
  • Maintain a fallback process when a third-party service is unavailable.

8. QA and launch acceptance checklist

  • Submit valid and invalid test enquiries from desktop and mobile.
  • Verify contact fields, source attribution, tags, owner, opportunity and task creation.
  • Confirm the correct welcome message is triggered once and uses accurate fields.
  • Reply to a message and verify the relevant follow-up sequence stops or changes as specified.
  • Test opt-out/suppression behavior and ensure further marketing messages are not sent through the affected channel.
  • Book, reschedule and cancel a test appointment; verify calendar status and reminder handling.
  • Test no-show, attended, won and lost outcomes and confirm the correct pipeline/report updates.
  • Test AI known-answer, unknown-answer, booking and human-handoff scenarios.
  • Test integration failures, invalid payloads and duplicate webhook events where applicable.
  • Reconcile dashboard counts against the underlying test records and document known limitations.
  • Obtain JEFT FIT approval of templates, routing rules, user access and launch readiness.

9. Deliverables & handover

  • Audit summary with current-state observations and recommended changes.
  • Documented contact fields, tags, pipeline stages and stage definitions.
  • Configured or refined workflows and message templates within the agreed scope.
  • Integration mapping and notes for supported connected services.
  • AI assistant instructions, approved knowledge boundaries and escalation behavior if included.
  • Test checklist with outcomes, exceptions and remaining dependencies.
  • Dashboard/KPI definitions and a maintenance checklist.
  • Walkthrough meeting and handover notes for the staff responsible for day-to-day use.

10. Assumptions, dependencies & exclusions

  • JEFT FIT provides authorized access to the relevant GHL location, website/funnel, calendar and connected services.
  • JEFT FIT approves the sales stages, routing rules, message content, consent process and AI knowledge.
  • Third-party subscription charges, phone/SMS usage, AI usage, paid integrations and provider fees are not assumed to be included.
  • Any required business verification, messaging approval, domain authentication or external provider setup may affect readiness.
  • Migration of large historical datasets, custom software development, ad campaign management and unsupported third-party features are outside this concept unless explicitly agreed.
  • Existing systems should be reviewed before recommending a rebuild; final architecture may change based on audit findings.
  • No price, delivery timeline or performance guarantee is included in this concept proposal. Scope, commercial terms and launch plan can be discussed after a discovery meeting.

Agree the highest-priority issue, identify dependencies, and determine the smallest reliable first release. The implementation plan should be finalized only after the existing account and requirements are understood.
